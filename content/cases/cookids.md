## The problem

**Our question:** how can children's digital consumption support their self-actualisation?

- **Picky eating starts with the eyes:** children refuse food they do not visually approve of
- **Persuasion does not work:** repeated persuasion by parents is often ineffective
- **Food education is boring:** it cannot hold children's attention

**Our answer:** quiz-based interaction to spark children's interest, and cooking together with parents so picky eating improves naturally. The name sounds like "cool kids": kids who enjoy cooking and eat well are the real cool kids.

## Research

**Desk research** pointed the same way: gamified nutrition education raised children's knowledge and fruit and vegetable intake, digital tools co-designed with parents increased kids' motivation to eat healthily, and cooking with parents helped children try new foods.

**Affinity mapping** of problems, user needs, users and value proposition led to four insights:

- **Cooking together:** children are more willing to try new foods when they cook with their parents
- **Bigger screens:** a tablet holds children's focus better than a phone
- **Repetition:** repeated exposure to the same foods reduces picky eating over time
- **Play:** gamified food activities and familiar characters raise children's interest

**Persona:** Rachel, 29, a freelance marketer in Berlin with a four-year-old son. New to parenting, she wants simple recipes and quality time, and finds most parenting and cooking apps complicated or not kid-friendly.

## The concept

- **Goals menu:** the parent picks the ingredients the child should eat more of
- **Quizzes:** the child plays a quiz that leads to a matching recipe, so the parent's goal and the child's interest meet
- **Smart matching:** personalised recipe suggestions
- **Tracking:** logs which ingredients were used and gives feedback
- **Progress check:** parents see how the child's eating improves

**What changed along the way**

| From | To | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Mobile app | Tablet | A bigger screen holds children's attention |
| Recommendations from simple quizzes | Recommendations linked to the Goals menu | Connects the parent's intention with the child's interests |

| Open question | Our answer |
|---|---|
| How hard should quizzes be? | Split into stages |
| How to define how much to eat? | Draw on nutritionist input |
| How to measure improvement? | Count how often each ingredient is eaten |
| Could the Goals menu push the parent's bias? | Quiz-based suggestions keep the child's choice |

## Cooka, the bear chef

I created Cooka, the guide children cook with. With Cooka's help, kids explore fun, healthy recipes and build confidence in the kitchen, following each recipe step by step.

## What we learned

The hardest question was balance: the parent sets the goal, but the child has to feel the choice is theirs. Linking quiz-based suggestions to the Goals menu was our answer.
