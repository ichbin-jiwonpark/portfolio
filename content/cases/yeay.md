## Background

Quality education is one of the UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDG 4). Our starting point was mentoring: research we reviewed shows that its benefits go beyond grades. Mentees gain self-esteem, confidence and interpersonal skills; mentors gain self-esteem, a sense of accomplishment and patience. Structured mentoring relationships, such as weekly one-to-one meetings, can also help reduce anxiety and social-emotional difficulties.

Our question was how a platform could support young people's growth through peer mentoring, where the same person can be a learner in one subject and a teacher in another.

## Concept

The name takes the "ea" shared by t**ea**ch and l**ea**rn, and the joyful "yay" of growing. The core idea: **"Just like a rainbow grows, so does a child's personality."** Every mentoring activity turns into data, and that data is shown back as growth.

The service follows a simple loop: mentoring activities, data collection, visualisation, feedback and growth. For a 17-year-old persona who is new in town and wants to learn German and play football, the journey runs from profile setup to mentor recommendations, a scheduled learning plan, the mentoring sessions and a view of their own data.

## Flows and wireframes

My part was the user flows and wireframes: from setting up a profile and getting matched with a mentor, to chatting and agreeing on a time and place, an automatically generated learning path, sessions online or offline, homework check-ins and the data pages.

## Outcome

The final prototype shows growth in three ways: a rainbow for personality growth, clouds for the learning path and extra resources, and a data page with daily progress, time spent as a learner and as a teacher, and badges for the strengths the activities build. Credits earned through mentoring can be used for offers from partners.

This was my first design project, done in my first semester of the MA.
