## The problem

Fitness apps are built to track progress, but for many people they become a source of guilt. A missed day shows up as an empty ring or a broken streak, and rest simply does not count.

**My question:** how can a wellness app remove the emotional barriers that come with activity tracking?

## Research

**Three interviews** with people in their early to mid-30s who had used fitness apps and stopped. All three described the same thing.

> "It felt like I was exercising just to log it. The tool had become the goal." *Interviewee A*

- **Pressure to log:** people exercised to fill the record, not because they enjoyed it
- **Guilt on rest days:** an empty ring turns a rest day into a failure
- **No reward for keeping going:** logging led to nothing visible

**Six apps compared** on how they treat rest, gamification, guilt and notifications.

| App | Rest logging | Gamification | Guilt-free | Notifications |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Apple / Samsung Health | No | No | No | Pressure-based |
| Strava | No | Partial | No | Social comparison |
| Calm | No | No | No | Streak-based |
| Duolingo | No | Yes | Partial | Countdown, urgent |
| Finch | Yes | Yes | Partial | Emotional |
| **POOM** | **Yes** | **Yes** | **Yes** | **Affection-based** |

**The insight:** the problem is in the structure of the apps, not in people's motivation. Self-determination theory backs this up: fear-based engagement, such as streaks and penalties, works only in the short term.

## The concept

In poomsae, the forms of Taekwondo, no movement is skipped and each flows into the next. POOM carries that into tracking with one principle: **the flow never breaks.**

- **No empty days:** exercise (+3 XP), "I did something for me" (+2 XP) or "I did nothing" (+1 XP). After 24 hours without an answer, the day is recorded as a recharge day.
- **XP never goes down:** nothing to lose, no streak to break.
- **"Recharge", not "rest":** "I did nothing" comes with "And that's okay. It recharges you."
- **Equal weight:** exercise and recharge buttons have the same size and place, and the home screen shows no statistics.
- **Belts paced by habit research:** six belts, white to black; early steps are short so progress is felt early.
- **A landscape instead of a number:** the home scene grows with each belt, from an empty sky to a sky full of stars.
- **Rewards that value rest:** the first reward is a Rest Pass. Partner rewards from wellness platforms stay a concept for now.

## Testing

A 40-minute walkthrough of the prototype over Zoom with one interview participant.

- **Worked:** the core idea, including passive recharge, was understood without explanation; the belt logic made sense; the Taekwondo design language felt coherent
- **To improve:** the guilt-free idea was not visible enough on the home screen; chart and labels sat too far apart; synced activities need manual editing; rewards felt more commercial than the rest
- **Thesis defence:** one participant is too small a sample, and European users may need a short guide to poomsae and belts

## Next steps

Designing the right logic is one challenge; making people see it on screen is another. Next: test with more and more varied people, add a short guide to the Taekwondo ideas, and make the guilt-free idea visible on the home screen.
