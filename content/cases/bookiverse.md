## The problem

New writers struggle to be seen without an existing audience, and most reading platforms treat readers as passive consumers. Younger readers want to take part: give feedback, follow the process, support a writer.

**My question:** how might we help emerging authors gain visibility and connect with readers to grow together?

## Research

**Two interviews,** about 50 minutes each: an independent author who publishes on Brunch, a Korean writing platform, and an office worker who reads heavily and likes finding new writers.

> "Two genuinely posted comments are much more helpful than 200 likes." *The author*

> "It's so hard to spot a rookie writer. The platform doesn't actively support rookies." *The reader*

- **A discovery gap:** new writers and readers cannot find each other
- **Relationships drive both sides:** writers keep going when they know someone reads them
- **No growth system:** platforms show neither growth data nor a path to discovery
- **Demand to take part:** readers want to be part of the creative process

**Six platforms compared,** three global and three Korean. None combined author growth with active reader participation. That became the positioning.

| Platform | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Wattpad | Massive user base | Little curation, uneven quality |
| Inkitt | Data-driven selection | Low interactivity |
| Kindle Vella | Episodic format | Minimal feedback loop |
| Brunch | Great writing tools | Hard for emerging authors to be seen |

## The concept

Two journeys that meet at support: the reader discovers, reads, supports and gets a reward; the author publishes, reaches readers, gets support and grows.

![The reader journey and the author journey, connected at the support step](images/bookiverse/process.svg)

- **New authors first:** recommended books by new authors sit right below the reader's shelf
- **Read before you decide:** the first chapter is free, then "Do you like this story?"
- **Show what support brings:** extra chapters, the author's commentary and a goods kit, listed before anyone pays
- **Words, not only likes:** after supporting, the reader is asked for a written review, in the author's voice
- **Every ask can be skipped:** each prompt comes with "No, thanks" or "Maybe later"
- **A dashboard for the author:** members, reviews and support received, each with a graph

## Feedback and next steps

- **Professor:** called the structure and research path "very nice, very logical", and suggested developing the business model further, for example a subscription alongside reader support
- **Not yet tested:** next, I want one real author and one real reader to use the prototype
- **Revenue model:** reader support, a subscription, or both
